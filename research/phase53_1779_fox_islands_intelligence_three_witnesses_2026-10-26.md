# Phase 53 — Penobscot Expedition: Fox Islands intelligence and source limits (26 October 2026)

## Summary of the strongest newly traced research lead

A published 1975 U.S. Marine Corps historical study identifies an **actual interaction between American military officers and three unnamed local informants near the Fox Islands during the opening of the Penobscot Expedition in July 1779**. This is a **new sharply defined search target**, not proof of Thomas Beverage's participation.

### 24–25 July 1779: three local informants brought to the American flagship

Charles R. Smith, *Marines in the Revolution: A History of the Continental Marines in the American Revolution, 1775–1783* (History and Museums Division, Headquarters, U.S. Marine Corps, Washington, D.C., 1975), chapter on the Penobscot Expedition, reports:
- American fleet left Townsend on **24 July 1779** and anchored that evening near the **Fox Islands**.
- The brig **Hazard**, under Captain John Foster Williams, and the **Tyrannicide** were serving as scouting vessels.
- Second Lieutenant **William Cunningham** of the *Hazard* was sent ashore for intelligence about British forces; he returned with **three unnamed men** who were forwarded to Commodore **Dudley Saltonstall** on the flagship **Warren**.
- A separate boat obtained intelligence from Captain Mitchell of Belfast in Camden.

Accessible indexed excerpt: https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/Marines%20In%20The%20Revolution.pdf . The University of Marine Corps website currently refuses direct retrieval in some non-browser interfaces; **the original underlying ship log/report and the three men's names have not been examined**.

**Critical limitation:** The three men are *not named* in the available published passage. The passage does **not establish that they were Fox Islanders**, rather than residents elsewhere near the scouting location; it certainly does not establish that **Thomas Beverage** was one of them. Do not use it as individual service proof or substitute the family tradition for their names.

### Independent near-contemporary source for the Fox Islands stopover

Colonel Samuel McCobb's report of his 1779 expedition experience says his regiment arrived at **Fox Islands on 24 July 1779** and remained overnight; then proceeded toward Majabagaduce the next day. The report is cited to **Massachusetts Archives volume 145, page 54**, and appears as an edited transcription in *Maine Historical Magazine* 3 (1893), 'A True Relation of Facts Concerning the Penobscot Expedition: Colonel Samuel McCobb's Statement'; reprinted in *History of Bath and Environs, 1607–1894* (section on Penobscot expedition). The original Massachusetts manuscript is a verification target, not directly inspected for this note.

Accessible transcription: https://ldsgenealogy.com/books2/historyofbathenv1607reed.pdf (source PDF needs visual verification). A second textual reproduction: https://www.scribd.com/document/1006540901/Maine-Historical-Magazine-1893 .

This gives historically plausible geographic proximity to the Fox Island settlers named in the separate **1785 petition**, but no identity bridge.

### Fleet roster disambiguation: two different vessels called Polly

The scholarly *Journals of Ashley Bowen* edition (Colonial Society of Massachusetts), 1779 notes, quotes an original **'War Office, July 14th 1779' American vessel list, Marblehead Historical Society item 4229** (https://www.colonialsociety.org/node/740). The list names the brig *Hazard*, Captain John F. Williams; the ship *Warren*, Commodore Dudley Saltonstall; and the **6-gun sloop *Charming Polly*, commander John Palmer**.

The **1779 *Charming Polly*** cannot be silently identified with the **12-gun privateer sloop *Polly*** commissioned for Captain Nathaniel Leech in **September 1776**. The earlier vessel's ownership by William Blackler and James Mugford is documented in the *Journals of Ashley Bowen* editorial note 219, citing Gardner W. Allen, *Massachusetts Privateers of the Revolution*, p. 239 (https://www.colonialsociety.org/node/740). Neither roster provides a Thomas Beverage crew entry.

### Other targeted original repositories

- **Massachusetts Historical Society, 'Penobscot Expedition papers, 1778–1779', one folder**: lists of sailors and receipts of supplies from Joseph Noyes for *Hazard*, *Warren*, *Providence*, *Active* and other ships. ArchiveGrid item: https://researchworks.oclc.org/archivegrid/archiveComponent/22464041 . No crew lists independently read in this pass.
- **Massachusetts Archives vol. 145** (Penobscot Expedition) and **vol. 139** (Penobscot ship pay orders, *Pallas*, *Vengeance*, plus privateer bonds): official finding aid https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/collections/mass-archives-volume-list.htm .
- **NARA microfilm M804, John Newbury/Lucy Newbury rejected-pension case R7613**. A secondary genealogy says p.50 quotes **Benjamin Beverage** regarding John Newbury's 1824 death; original page not obtained in this pass. National Archives guide: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/army/pensions . The '*R*' prefix denotes a rejected latest application, not necessarily an absence of wartime service; do not conflate Newbury's claim with Thomas Beverage's own service.

## Priorities for actual discovery

1. Locate the **underlying 1779 scouting report, ship journal, or Saltonstall correspondence** for July 24–25 and determine whether any of the **three men interviewed** are named. **If names are found**, verify each identity independent of mere locality.
2. Locate **nominal pay and crew records** for the *Hazard* and participating militia / service at Penobscot; search all Beverage/Beveridge surname variants.
3. Obtain **Thomas Beverage's 30 June 1794 sworn deposition** from Suffolk Court Case Files 1796 and differentiate it from the unrelated 1796 writ/appearance notice already supplied. It may be chiefly a land/civil dispute, not military testimony.
4. Obtain the original **John Newbury R7613 page 50** to independently verify Benjamin Beverage's reported testimony.
5. Preserve priority of the still-unexamined **March 1865 Albert Beverage family letter**, requested from the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum by the researcher, without making duplicate contact.

## Evidence classification

- **Documented:** 1779 American expedition operated around Fox Islands and obtained intelligence from three unnamed men near its scouting area; contemporary 1785 Fox Islands petition lists Thomas Beveridge while collectively claiming some islanders' wartime armed service.
- **Not proven:** Thomas was one of those three men, served in the expedition, served aboard *Hazard*, *Warren* or *Charming Polly*, joined William Blackler's *Polly*, or attended the Boston Tea Party.
