# Phase 24 — Clarissa Cobb's Carver birth family, and the unresolved origins of Matthew Beveridge

**Research completed for this phase:** 19 October 2026  
**Scope:** targeted original-era and reliable published-source search back from Matthew's 1837 Waldo County estate, Hope land, and the 1785 Fox Islands petition. This report distinguishes **located source content** from an **archive finding aid** and a **specific historical instrument not yet accessed**.

## 1. A documented extension: Clarissa's birth family in Carver, Massachusetts

We found a **town-record-derived birth entry** in *Vital Records of Carver, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850*, New England Historic Genealogical Society, Boston (1911), **printed p. 21**, which lists:

> Clarissa, ch. Barnabas and Jerusha, Mar. 27, 1786.

The record occurs in the **COBB** birth listings; further children of the same parents are Hannah Crocker (1787), Sophia (1789), Ebenezer (1793), and Eleazer Crocker (1798). The pages are reproduced in transcription at https://genealogytrails.com/mass/plymouth/births-carver-to1850.html and a digitized print of the 1911 book is cataloged at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vital_records_of_Carver,_Massachusetts,_to_the_year_1850_(IA_vitalrecordsofca01carv).pdf.

This is historical vital-register-derived evidence of **Clarissa Cobb's parents, Barnabas and Jerusha**, *not* a statement that Clarissa became Matthew's wife. The actual handwritten Carver town ledger was **not independently inspected**; the printed compilation is an abstract/transcription of original town material, not an original 1786 birth certificate.

The crucial **identity bridge** comes from Philip L. Cobb, *A History of the Cobb Family*, Part I (1907), **printed pp. 164–165, entry 206**. This assigns Barnabas Cobb (reported born 1760) and Jerusha Cobb as parents of Clarissa, born 27 March 1786 in Carver, and states that she married **Capt. Matthew Beverage** and lived in Hope, Maine. Barnabas and Jerusha reportedly moved to Warren, Maine, in 1802; Barnabas died in 1807; Jerusha died in 1843. Read: https://archive.org/stream/historyofcobbfam13cobb/historyofcobbfam13cobb_djvu.txt (printed p. 164).

**Assessment: Strongly supported but not certified.** The parent-child relation *Carver Clarissa → Barnabas/Jerusha* comes from a register-derived birth entry, while identification *Carver Clarissa → Matthew's widow Clarissa of Hope* rests on Philip Cobb's later genealogy, compatible with 1837 probate evidence identifying her as Matthew's widow. Cobb references Eaton's **Warren** history elsewhere; do not overcount Eaton and Philip Cobb as independent original marriage witnesses. The exact Matthew–Clarissa wedding date/place remains unlocated.

### New tree treatment

The website's **separate Phase 24 genealogy overlay** adds Barnabas Cobb and Jerusha Cobb as parents of the previously documented widow Clarissa. Clarissa's Carver birth, name, and source notes are strengthened. Matthew's eight court-named children **remain connected to Matthew alone**: this finding does **not** assign their biological mother to Clarissa. An earlier immutable genealogy snapshot, 1837 probate manifest, and the already reviewed court-child data files remain intact.

### Candidate grandparents, not yet confirmed as tree edges

Philip Cobb's 1907 work additionally describes Jerusha Cobb as daughter of **Ebenezer Cobb III** and **Jerusha Cushman**. An older Cushman family genealogy similarly names a Jerusha Cushman born 15 January 1727/28, married Ebenezer Cobb, whose daughter Jerusha married Barnabas Cobb. See https://www.fourbranches.org/tng/folios/Cushman/A%20Historical%20and%20Biographical%20Genealogy%20of%20the%20Cushmans%20The%20Descendants%20of%20Robert%20Cushman%2C%20the%20Pur.pdf, Section on Jerusha Cushman.

This is an attractive potential *second* extension into the 1720s, but the relevant original baptism/marriage/probate details were not read from their record registers and repeating Cobb names make conflation possible. Therefore the claimed grandparents are displayed as **research leads only**, not automatically inserted into the family graph.

## 2. A separate Carver clue: Barnabas Cobb's municipal office

Henry S. Griffith's *History of the Town of Carver, Massachusetts: Historical Review, 1637–1910* (1913), printed pp. 328–335, includes a **Barnabas Cobb** in its early Carver town-clerk roster **1799–1801** and assessors list in the 1790s. https://ldsgenealogy.com/MA/books/History-of-the-town-of-Carver-Massachusetts-historical-review-1637-1910-part-17.htm.

This aligns chronologically with the 1907 genealogy's reported family move to Maine in 1802. **But the surname and timing do not themselves prove the listed officeholder is Clarissa's father.** The actual town appointment minutes and any migration deed must be checked.

## 3. The Matthew identity test: three records, no proved join

| Candidate | Time | What directly connects the person? | Remaining obstacle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Matthew Beveridge, a minor**, on 1785 Fox Islands petition | 1785 | Later transcript lists this name as a minor, as well as other Beverage/Beveridge names | No inspected original petition or precise parental/kinship statement |
| **Matthew Beveridge, clerk of Hope** | 1811–1827 | One Hope municipal town-ledger transcription repeatedly attributes clerk signatures to Matthew; separate 1820 census-index lead | No verified autograph and no record naming his wife, child, prior home, or father |
| **Matthew Beverage/Beveridge of Hope, deceased** | 1837 | Court probate with named widow **Clarissa** and eight individuals explicitly called **children** (October 1837 division warrant) | Court record does not identify birth parents or say that this decedent is the 1785 petition minor |

The names, regional geography and chronology are suggestive, but a genealogist should not create a father, brother or unique historical-person bridge solely on those agreements. No birth year for Matthew has been established from this research. The purported brother **Capt. Thomas Beverage** remains an unproven connection.

### New actionable archive identification: Rufus Putnam's survey

David Flanagan and Barbara Beverage Reeves, *The Ebenezer Eames Family: Early Settlers of North Fox Island, Maine*, **The Maine Genealogist 47(4), November 2025, pp. 170–182**, explains the **1 June 1785** islander petition, the commissioned **1786 Putnam survey**, and residents' declarations of when they had settled. The paper explicitly cites **General Rufus Putnam's report, dated 18 March 1786, paper collection Box 001, Maine State Archives, Augusta** (p. 170, note 6). https://maineroots.org/images/Nov25TMG2.pdf.

This is a precise *original-document retrieval lead*, not a viewed Matthew entry. It could show whether a named Matthew appears and any original-origin or settlement information. The paper also highlights that the **1800 census asked where island households had emigrated from**; checking the underlying original schedule should help distinguish families, not turn an island family into an assumed cousin.

## 4. Deed-chain investigation: coverage confirmed, deed not found

Prior probate scan **02** contains a homestead description and tentative prior-owner readings including Nathaniel Alford, Lucy Whitney, a Henry [surname unclear] and a George [surname unclear]. None of these names has been validated against an actual Hope land instrument.

- [Lincoln County registry index book coverage](https://help.searchiqs.com/article/93-melin): early index books cover 1761–1847, deed images from 1761.
- [Waldo County registry coverage](https://help.searchiqs.com/article/95-mewal): grantor/grantee books from 1827, deeds imaged from 1828.
- [Hope Historical Society town-map project](https://hopehist.com/Himages/XMay22.html): earlier original-lot work; requires a **proven deed lot number** before any modern map attribution.

**Negative-search boundary:** public web results and published finding aids did not yield a legible original **Matthew-specific instrument with a volume-and-page reference**. We cannot conclude no deed exists: some indexes are handwritten images behind an interactive search interface. Nothing in this phase invents a date of migration to Hope, a deed seller, land coordinates, or an inherited connection to Capt. Thomas.

## 5. Specific retrieval queue to resolve the remaining question

**First:** Get the original 1786 Putnam survey Box 001 and the actual 1785 petition entry; independently capture page, full person name, declared settlement years, location, neighboring list names and any household/guardian wording.

**Second:** Read Carver's original town birth register for the 1786 Clarissa entry and an original marriage return/intentions for Capt. Matthew and Clarissa Cobb. The Carver Town Records image access is described by [Massachusetts GenWeb's Carver guide](https://www.plymouthcolony.net/magenweb/plymouthcounty/carver/index.html), though image access can require a FamilySearch login.

**Third:** Use Lincoln **grantee/grantor** indexes, then Waldo from 1827, for all Beveridge/Beverage spelling variants. Do not count found index matches until the *book and page* are known and the recorded instrument states exact parties, date and bounds. Cross-check any alleged Alford/Whitney earlier seller with the 1837 probate appraisal.

**Fourth:** Determine whether Matthew's 1800–1820 appearances can be linked through a wife, taxed property, a child name, and civic roles, instead of through same-name assumption.

## Outcome

- **New supported branch:** Clarissa Cobb (1786 Carver) → Barnabas Cobb and Jerusha Cobb, connected to widow Clarissa and Matthew via the 1907 compiled identity bridge.
- **Additional possible maternal generation:** Jerusha's proposed parents Ebenezer Cobb III and Jerusha Cushman, not yet inserted as certain.
- **New original target:** exact 1786 Putnam archival box and report date, with the article explaining petition and early census methodology.
- **Still open:** Matthew's own parents, proposed brother Capt. Thomas, 1785 minor identity, original marriage registration, deed volumes and exact parcel boundary.

No probate image redistribution, correspondence, purchase, record addition with unsourced biological maternity, or mutation to protected baseline sources occurred.
