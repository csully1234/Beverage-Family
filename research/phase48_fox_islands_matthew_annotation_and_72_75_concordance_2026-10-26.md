# Phase 48 — manuscript correction and printed-edition concordance (26 October 2026)

## Research alert: age notation beside Matthew Beveridge

The original Massachusetts Archives manuscript (SC1 / Series 228, February 1785 session, Chapter 97; petition leaf 3, right-hand bottom of inhabitants list) shows **Matthew Beveridge** followed by a handwritten **“a minor.”** Unlike the 1889 printed transcription, the original page ALSO has a separate handwritten mark/word directly underneath an underline, which **appears to read “major.”**

**Do not resolve this as “Matthew was a major/adult” or “Matthew was definitively a minor.”** Potential explanations include later correction, comment on another context, scribal gloss, marginal amendment, or a misreading. The hands, ink sequence, editorial signs and date of the lower mark have **not** been independently established. The printed 1889 version reproduces only the *a minor* label.

| Observation | Evidentiary weight |
|---|---|
| “Matthew Beveridge” as a name in the 1785 petition | Directly visible original manuscript |
| “a minor” immediately beside his name | Directly visible first age annotation |
| Additional lower note that appears to read “major” | Tentative paleographic reading; not independently confirmed |
| Matthew's actual age/majority in 1785 | **Unknown** |
| Relationship to James, Thomas, or Matthew of Hope (1837) | **Unknown** |
| 1889 *Brief Historical Sketch* “Matthew Beveridge (a minor)” | Later printed transcription that does not discuss apparent lower annotation |

This matters to genealogical identity reconstruction because the earlier shorthand “minor” was being used to infer that Matthew must have been young in 1785; such birth-year constraints are **not safe** until the annotation is resolved.

**Recommended verification:** Obtain archival staff/qualified paleographer review of the original petition TIFF. Ask whether the “major” word was crossed out, inserted later, or refers to a corrected status. Compare the exact line with another certified copy of SC1 Series 228 Chapter 97, the petition subscribers' filed duplicate if any, and any legislative amendment minute referencing the petitioner. Do not infer date or writers from relative ink darkness alone.

## Primary-versus-printed discrepancy: 72 versus 75

**Original manuscript, resolution leaf 1, Senate March 11, 1786:**

> …whereon were **seventy two** settlers before the first day of January 1784…

**1889 Vinalhaven book, printed p. 10, presenting this same legislative resolve:**

> …whereon were **seventy-five** settlers before the first day of January, 1784…

This is a direct textual discrepancy **in the same sentence**, not a valid explanation that one figure is a petition-head count and the other an eligible-settlers count. The manuscript is the better witness to the wording of the adopted document in its present form. The historical *reason* for the different count remains unresolved; it could be typographic, editorial, or reflect another copy, but no cause is yet documented.

The survey acreage **16,527** and cutoff **January 1, 1784** agree across the manuscript excerpt and the later printing. The manuscript grants only to settlers who met the actual conditions, so this is not a list of 72 proven individual titles. It also mentions a conditional payment of **£108 in specie and £66 7s. in consolidated securities**, subject to original precise legal wording and annotations.

## New external corroborating clue to investigate

Flanagan and Barbara Beverage Reeves, “The Ebenezer Eames Family: Early Settlers of North Fox Island, Maine,” *The Maine Genealogist* 47, no. 4 (November 2025), pp. 170–171, references individual statements of settlement years made in the March 18, 1786 report of Rufus Putnam. The article reports specific figures for Eames-family and Dyer-family early settlers. That makes the **unexamined original report** particularly promising for James, Thomas and Matthew Beveridge; it does not mean those names or dates have already been established in that source.

Source citation: https://maineroots.org/images/Nov25TMG2.pdf (PDF pages 27–28; printed pp. 170–171, especially p. 170 n. 6).

Maine State Archives's public digital portal advertises over a million free digitized individual records, including land records and early-era county records, but no verified image of the exact Putnam Box 001 report has been located in the completed search. https://www3.maine.gov/sos/archives/digital-archives

## Next archival acquisition targets

1. **Maine State Archives** — “General Putnam's Report and Description in the County of Lincoln …” dated March 18, 1786, Box 001 (paper). Obtain its entire Fox Islands claim list, not only a surname extract; preserve page numbers and nearby grantees.
2. **Massachusetts Archives** — SC1 / Series 228, Passed Resolves, February 1785 session, Chapter 97. Ask staff to interpret Matthew's *a minor / major [?]* insertion and verify the number *seventy two* against surviving legislative engrossments.
3. **1786–1820 land grant and county deed chain** — locate actual conveyances and occupants for James and Thomas; guardianship and later marriage/land signatures for Matthew, without automatically identifying the 1837 Hope man.
4. **Census and town minutes** — use the exact 1790 enumeration and 1800 place-of-origin schedules as geographically contextual evidence, not as parentage proof.

**No messages have been sent to any archive.** This is a research and editorial annotation release, not a finding of a previously unknown genealogical relationship.
