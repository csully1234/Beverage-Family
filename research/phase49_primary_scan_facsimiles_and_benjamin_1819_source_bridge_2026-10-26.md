# Phase 49 — Original Fox Islands manuscript facsimiles and Benjamin Beverage's contemporary island testimony

**Investigation and site integration:** October 26, 2026.

## A substantial access change: the original 1785–1786 papers can now be viewed ON the website

Seven supplied original manuscript TIFFs from Massachusetts Archives, `SC1 / Series 228, Passed Resolves, February 1785 session, Chapter 97`, have been converted into color web-access SVG facsimiles (embedded, optimized JPEG derivatives). Four images belong to the settlers' petition and three to the legislative resolve. The website can display these pages within the existing Fox Islands Originals visitor section, support page-by-page navigation and export an individual facsimile.

These **are scans of the original handwritten documents**, not reconstructed illustrations. They are **optimized derivatives**, not the uncompressed 27–29 MB archival TIFF masters. The original TIFFs remain preserved in the conversation's research archive and were not added to the GitHub repository. Do not claim that the website serves the full-resolution master or a fully proofread diplomatic transcription.

The original petition names **James Beveridge**, **Thomas Beveridge**, **Matthew Beveridge**. Beside Matthew, the underlined phrase `a minor` and a second lower phrase tentatively read as `major` mean age is unresolved. No familial edge, birth date or 1837 Hope identity is proved by either label.

The March 1786 original resolve states **seventy two** early settlers and **16,527 acres**, versus the 1889 printed town history's **seventy-five** in the *same clause*. The original manuscript is currently the strongest witness for the resolve's wording; the historical reason for the mismatch is unresolved. The grant cutoff was January 1, 1784, but it is not an independent proof that a particular Beveridge entry fulfilled the grant conditions.

## Benjamin Beverage's remarkable contemporary letter: what changed

The record **was already in this website's catalog since September 2026** as `src_vinalhaven_manuscript_1819`, and already linked provisionally to `benjamin_kent_beverage_1779`. We did **not** newly discover the archival item's existence and should not suggest otherwise. What is new is the **first full two-page manuscript-and-transcription examination in this site workflow**, the thematic source analysis, cross-source chronology, and prominent visitor navigation.

**Holding institution:** Maine Historical Society, William D. Williamson Papers, Coll. 62, Maine Memory Network #20801. **Document date:** December 20, 1819. **Creator:** Benjamin Beverage (as signed). **Original:** ink on paper, two leaves. **Host page:** https://www.mainememory.net/record/20801. **Full two-page manuscript + transcription at custodian:** https://www.mainememory.net/media/pdf/20801.pdf.

The original letter's topics, verified against the institution's hosted manuscript images and typed transcription:

- **Community origin:** Benjamin reports first settlement in 1765 and says the name Vinalhaven was given in June 1789 for Boston's John Vinal, without residents requesting or welcoming the name.
- **Land/title:** The inhabitants held land under a Massachusetts General Court grant, the town was surveyed in 1785, and Benjamin rounded the town area to about 17,000 acres. Contrast with the distinct 1786 resolution's exact 16,527 acres. This is not proof of a particular family's deed.
- **Trade and agriculture:** Benjamin describes mostly fishing vessels, three stores, saw and grist mills, and crop yields.
- **Schools:** Eight districts and roughly 300 scholars between four and sixteen were described, with around $400 annually appropriated for schooling.
- **Religious life:** Baptist and Methodist congregations with voluntary minister support.
- **Historical authorial caveat:** The original closes with a statement that his name should not be used as an authority. The site preserves this as a reminder not to treat his recollections as self-proving.

The original publication host marks images usable for educational purposes while restricting broader reproduction; **do not copy/rehost either Maine Memory Network manuscript page image or its full transcription**. The website links the institutional original and paraphrases selected points, rather than importing copyrighted/rights-limited images.

## 1790 census household and 1800 migration question

The site's existing Phase 45 research already recorded a later printed enumeration for **Thomas Beverege** in 1790 Vinalhaven: 2 free white males aged 16+, 4 younger males, 5 females, 11 individuals total. The source remains a later transcription, not a verified read of the handwritten census sheet; the unnamed 10 co-residents must not be linked as a particular spouse or child.

The 1800 census reportedly included a special `from whence emigrated` column, indexed in Walter Goodwin Davis, “Part of Hancock County, Maine, in 1800,” *NEHGR* 105 (1951):204–213, 276–291. Although this could establish a previous place of residence for an actual Beveridge family, **no Beveridge-specific response has yet been verified**, and Marshfield/Martha's Vineyard migration of unrelated Eames households is not proof for the Beverage family.
