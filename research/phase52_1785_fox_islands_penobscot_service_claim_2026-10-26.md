# Phase 52 — Thomas Beveridge and the 1785 Fox Islands claim of wartime armed service

**Research date:** 2026-10-26.  
**Evidence policy:** Original names and collective language must not be converted into an individually proven Revolutionary War service record.

## Principal finding: a contemporary collective claim, not an individual muster roll

Four original 1785 Fox Islands petition leaves are already exhibited in this repository. Two leaves read together establish an important new *source connection*:

1. **Petition leaf 1:** The inhabitants' petition to the Massachusetts Senate and House states that, after British troops took Bagaduce, inhabitants unwilling to submit to British rule removed with their families into the protection of Massachusetts and **"did serve during the Expeditions to Penobscott bearing Arms against the enemy"** (spelling/capitalization approximates the hand). The same passage says that, after the defeat of Massachusetts troops, many settlers lost homes/property and some were compelled to work on British fortifications.
2. **Petition leaf 3:** The accompanying, apparently single-clerk-hand inhabitants list includes **"Thomas Beveridge"** in the right column, **"James Beveridge"** in the left column, and **"Matthew Beveridge"** with a contested underlined "a minor" and later-looking "major" annotation. Legislative endorsements on the leaf are dated **June 1, 1785**.

**Document identity:** Massachusetts Archives, SC1 / Series 228, Passed Resolves, February 1785 session, Chapter 97; four petition leaves (with associated March 1786 resolve). The legislative session series date and a specific manuscript endorsement date must not be conflated.

### Precisely what follows from the combination

- **Established from the original:** A person recorded as **Thomas Beveridge** is among the Fox Islands inhabitants named in the 1785 petition, and the petition presents a collective account of some inhabitants serving in Penobscot operations while opposing the British.
- **Not established:** That Thomas personally enlisted, bore arms, fought in the 1779 assault, served aboard a privateer, held a military captaincy, or was physically present at Castine. The list explicitly includes a **widow (Penelope Winslow)** and individuals noted as **minors**, proving that the military-service assertion cannot mean every person listed was a combatant.
- **Identity caution:** The petition's Thomas is a strong geographical/chronological candidate for the later North Fox Island town clerk, but the entry has no age, parentage, identifiable autograph signature or unique identifier; record the occurrence rather than automatically joining genealogy nodes.
- **Source-dependence caution:** The 1889 *Brief Historical Sketch of the Town of Vinalhaven* reproduces the petition; a later newspaper quotation of the same passage is not an independent second contemporary claim. The 1821 account below might also have drawn on local records.

## Additional sources checked

### Thomas Waterman to William D. Williamson, April 1821

- Original handwritten historical account catalogued by Maine Memory Network as [MMN 20802](https://www.mainememory.net/record/20802); [five-page transcription PDF](https://www.mainememory.net/media/pdf/20802.pdf), Maine Historical Society, William D. Williamson papers, Coll. 62.
- Manuscript opening states **April 5, 1821**; catalog metadata says **April 21, 1821**. Preserve the discrepancy until the autograph date and catalog are reconciled.
- Describes forced fort labor, abandoned/destroyed property and coerced allegiance in connection with 1779 British occupation; it does **not** assign combat service to Thomas individually.
- Specifically records **"Delegate to the Convention at Brunswick, Capt Thomas Beverage"** and describes **"Capt Thomas Beverage"** as second plantation clerk and later town clerk. This confirms his captain title was used during his lifetime but not that it was a military rank.
- Rights note: MMN marks the transcription for educational use and reserves reproduction rights for images; link to the holding institution rather than embedding scans without clearance.

### Benjamin Beverage, December 20, 1819

- [Maine Memory Network MMN 20801](https://www.mainememory.net/record/20801); [transcription](https://www.mainememory.net/media/pdf/20801.pdf). A family-authored contemporary description of Fox Islands settlement, agriculture, fishing vessels and local institutions, signed Benjamin Beverage.
- Benjamin's settlement year **1765** differs from Waterman's **1766**. Neither should silently overwrite the other.
- The letter does **not** independently corroborate Thomas's Boston Tea Party participation or privateering.

### Colonel Samuel McCobb's 1779 expedition account

- A retrospective printed transcription of a contemporary account reports McCobb's regiment and transports **arrived at the Fox Islands on July 24, 1779** and stayed overnight before proceeding toward Bagaduce. The publication identifies the original as **Massachusetts Archives, Volume 145, page 54**.
- Printed version: *Maine Historical Magazine* (1893), "A True Relation of Facts Concerning the Penobscot Expedition: Colonel Samuel McCobb's Statement," reportedly contributed by Joseph Williamson; also reprinted in *History of Bath and Environs, 1607–1894*.
- This puts an identifiable part of the **American expedition** at Fox Islands. It does **not** identify Thomas among soldiers or sailors and should not be presented as if it does.
- Printed transcription reference: https://ldsgenealogy.com/books2/historyofbathenv1607reed.pdf (the original Volume 145 folio has not been inspected in this phase).

### New targeted archival collection

- [Massachusetts Historical Society, Penobscot Expedition papers, 1778–1779, 1 folder (ArchiveGrid)](https://researchworks.oclc.org/archivegrid/archiveComponent/22464041).
- Description reports *lists of sailors and receipts for provisions* supplied by Joseph Noyes for the 1779 expedition, referencing the brig **Hazard**, ship **Warren**, sloop **Providence**, brig **Active**, and other vessels.
- This is more targeted than general FamilySearch search results. Exact crew names and contents **have not been read**. A matching sailor entry would require identity analysis; absence would not exclude other militia or privately operated vessels.
- The [Massachusetts Archives volume guide](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/collections/mass-archives-volume-list.htm) documents Volume 145 (Penobscot Expedition) and Volume 139 (Penobscot-related payments, privateer bonds).
- The University of New Brunswick's [Loyalist Collection guide](https://loyalist.lib.unb.ca/record/massachusetts-archives-collection-1603-1789) further reports crew-pay orders in Vol. 139 for brig **Pallas** and ship **Vengeance**, with names that can include rank, residence and relatives. These are targets to inspect, **not Thomas discoveries**.

## What would settle the individual claim

Best evidence: a **named, dateable contemporary muster roll, pay voucher, vessel/crew list, officer's statement, prize-court deposition, or service reimbursement** identifying Thomas Beverage/Beveridge as participant and connecting him geographically or genealogically to North Fox Island / Marblehead.

A collective 1785 assertion + inhabitant-name list raises the priority of this search materially; it is not a substitute for named personal-service evidence.
