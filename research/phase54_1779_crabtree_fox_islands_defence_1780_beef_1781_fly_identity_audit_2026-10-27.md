# Phase 54 — Fox Islands Revolutionary maritime defense: Crabtree muster, displaced settlers' provisioning, and the *Fly* (27 October 2026)

## Executive finding and standard of proof

**New targeted lead:** A small **named, official Massachusetts sea-coast defense company** served specifically at **Fox Islands and adjacent places between 5 September and 5 December 1779** under **Captain Eleazer Crabtree**. The original nominal payroll for **all nine men** is now the highest-priority service-record target for **Thomas Beverage/Beveridge (ca. 1750–1834)**. This period **follows** the failed July–August 1779 American assault on Castine; defense duty must not be conflated with participation in the expedition.

**Current limitation:** The actual 1779 roster/payroll **has not been found or inspected**. The nine-person unit is proved by published government compilation and local history, **not** proof Thomas enlisted. A family researcher separately says **Joseph Woster/Wooster**, Mercy Kent Beverage's brother-in-law, was among the defenders; this claim requires the underlying military record.

**Independent contextual finding:** A March 20, **1780** Massachusetts petition says some displaced **Fox Island and Long Island residents supplied beef to American troops** during the Penobscot campaign. The petitioner was named **Waterman Thomas** (Waterman **given name**, Thomas **surname**) and was a commissary/quartermaster. This **does not** identify Captain Thomas Beverage or prove he furnished beef. Do NOT misread "Waterman Thomas" as "Thomas Waterman" or confuse either with Thomas Beverage.

## A. September–December 1779: the Crabtree Fox Islands defense force

1. **Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth**, *Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War: A Compilation from the Archives*, **volume 4 (C volume), printed p. 59**, original listing under **CRABTREE, ELEAZER**:

   > Captain; engaged Aug. 8, 1775; service to Dec. 31, 1775, 5 mos. 5 days, at Frenchman's Bay; also, Captain of a Seacoast co.; entered service Sept. 5, 1779; discharged Dec. 5, 1779; company raised for defence of Fox Islands and adjacent places.

   **Source:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dc/Massachusetts_soldiers_and_sailors_of_the_revoluntionary_war._A_compilation_from_the_archives_%28IA_cu31924092740004%29.pdf . This is a late-19th-century *official archival compilation* and is **not the original payroll**. It independently verifies the service unit and time span.

2. **Cyrus Eaton**, *Annals of the Town of Warren* (19th-century history; section on 1779), records **"nine men, under Capt. Eleazer Crabtree and Sergeant David Jenks"** serving from **5 September to 5 December** for the defense of Fox Islands. The original cited evidence is **"Pay-rolls in Sec. Office"**; that is an actionable provenance clue:
   https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/annalsoftownofwa00lceato/annalsoftownofwa00lceato_bw.pdf

3. **Barbara Beverage Reeves**, genealogical research note preserved by FamilySearch, identifies **Joseph Woster/Wooster** as a private in **Eleazer Crabtree's naval company** from **5 September to 5 December 1779**, reportedly with service on three vessels, one called *Boston*. Her note identifies him as the husband of **Ann Kent**, a sister of **Mercy Kent**, wife of Thomas Beverage:
   https://www.familysearch.org/en/memories/memory/186471011/Notes%2Bfrom%2Bfindagravecom

4. The full nominal roll may establish whether Thomas Beverage served, **even if his name was absent from the printed alphabetic index**. But a negative result on this specific roll alone would not disprove other independent service or privateering. Search for Thomas / Thos / Beverage / Beveridge / Beveredge / Bevridge on the actual list, *not* merely by captain name in FamilySearch full-text.

**Request description for Massachusetts Archives if the researcher's already-sent inquiry receives a reply:** "Original Sept. 5–Dec. 5, 1779 seacoast company pay/muster roll, Captain Eleazer Crabtree, Sergeant David Jenks, nine men for Fox Islands and adjacent places; 19th-century state military compilation vol. 4, p. 59; Cyrus Eaton cites 'Pay-rolls in Sec. Office.' Request full original folios, call numbers, and payroll entries for all nine men." No duplicate inquiry should be sent without user approval.

### Time clarification

- **24 July – August 1779**: Massachusetts-led **Penobscot Expedition** against British at Castine; failed by 14 August.
- **5 September – 5 December 1779**: Crabtree's **Fox Islands coastal defense detail**. It is **post-defeat protection**, not automatically an expedition contingent.
- **1781**: Another local armed-boat operation (*Fly*), separately commissioned and **not the same unit**.

## B. March 20, 1780: independent contemporary evidence of Fox Islands provisioning

*The Baxter Manuscripts* (*Documentary History of the State of Maine*, volume published with archival transcriptions; printed p. **145**), reproduces a memorial of **Waterman Thomas**, Eastern Department commissary, dated **Boston, 20 March 1780**.

The original-era passage states that displaced inhabitants of **Penobscot, Fox Island & Long Island** provided a large share of beef for American troops, often out of supplies saved from the enemy; the memorial asks Massachusetts to settle the cost fairly because these inhabitants had suffered major losses. This is a separate original-era assertion supporting the **regional logistics and humanitarian hardship** described in the Fox Islands **1785 collective petition**.

**Exact source:** https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/baxtermanuscript11baxt/baxtermanuscript11baxt.pdf (printed p. 145). The page has been read from indexed text; the original manuscript and a visually checked printed page have **not** been separately compared.

**Crucial identity correction:** "Waterman Thomas" is the full name of a documented commissary (Waterman Thomas, born 1753 in Marshfield, per separately cited family material), **NOT Thomas Waterman** (different Fox Islands historian) and **NOT Thomas Beverage**. No individual's delivery or quantity is named in this excerpt.

**Follow-up original:** Locate the 1779–1780 **Waterman Thomas Penobscot Expedition beef procurement accounts, vouchers, claimants or receipts**, which might name Fox Island vendors (perhaps including Beverage). The Memorial is a pointer to such **named** receipts, not the receipts themselves. An archive contents list mentions Waterman Thomas's Aug. 21, 1780 memorial as Eastern troops commissary (https://loyalist.lib.unb.ca/sites/default/files/Massachusetts%20Archives%20Content%20List.pdf), but the complete claim packet has not been inspected.

## C. Boat *Fly*: documented 1781 private-armed-boat commission; serious John Perry identity problem

1. A genealogical history reproduces a purported **certified extract** from **Massachusetts Archives vol. 171, p. 487**: petition for **John Perry** to command armed boat *Fly*, to interrupt trade with British at Bagaduce, approved by Council **21 August 1781**. It also transcribes a subsequent petition to arm another small boat with **10 men** for joint operation with Perry. The apparent petitioners identify themselves as **"Inhabitants of St. George"**; signatories include **JOHN PERRY** and **THOMAS THOMPSON**.
   
   Source reproduction: https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G005068.pdf , printed p. 11.
   
2. A separate *Maine Privateers* study lists **John Perry of St. Georges** commanding the *Fly* from **22 August 1781**, reportedly armed with small arms and **9 men**, and again **29 June 1782** with one gun and 14 men. This **later study** describes him as **born 1750, died 1833**.
   
   Source: https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/346217866.pdf , printed p. 219. The full text/underlying original was **not** inspected beyond indexed passage.

3. The **1889 Vinalhaven** historical sketch describes a **John Perry of the Fox Islands**, **born 1741**, who settled on Crabtree's Point and supposedly died **1842**, and a later family history attributes *Fly* service to him:
   https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/briefhistoricals00vinl/briefhistoricals00vinl.pdf (printed pp. 55–56);
   https://ldsgenealogy.com/ME/books/Genealogical-and-family-history-of-the-state-of-Maine-Volume-IV-part-60.htm .

**Identity conflict:** The **St. George** commander apparently born **1750/died 1833** is not yet reconciled with the **Fox Islands** settler said to be **born 1741/died 1842**. These may be **two men called John Perry** or incompatible later biographical traditions. Therefore **do not identify the *Fly* commander as Thomas Beverage's Fox Islands neighbor until an original contemporary record bridges the men**. It would be a significant genealogical error to join them solely because of the common name.

**Priority originals:** Massachusetts Archives **vol. 171, p. 487**, adjacent 1781 petitions/commission endorsements; June 29, 1782 *Fly* commission; original surety/privateer bonds and pay/crew lists naming *Fly* personnel. Must check distinguishing residence, spouse, landholding, age, captain's signature, or later probate data.

## D. 1785 property witness evidence for Thomas and Mercy; companion of 1779 defender

**Barbara Beverage Reeves**, the genealogist noted above, cites **7 November 1785** deed, **Hancock/Knox Registry vol. 2, p. 22, deed no. 213**, recorded in **1792**, involving **Anthony Dyer**, **Joseph Woster** and **Levi Smith**; she reports **Thomas Beverage and Mercy (Kent) Beverage as witnesses**.

This would place Thomas and Mercy in a **contemporary legal association with Joseph Woster**, whom her research identifies as a member of Crabtree's 1779 defense company. It is a **promising original-document retrieval target**, not a firsthand verification of the deed or of Woster's muster: both claims presently derive from the same researcher's description. The exact grantor–grantee phrasing should be checked on the original before copying any deed summary.

- Researchers: inspect recorded deed **vol. 2, p. 22, deed 213** and adjoining page(s).
- The family relationship (Mercy/Ann sisters) should be verified by contemporaneous Kent records.
- This association **does not** imply Thomas served alongside Woster in 1779.

## E. Three July 1779 military intelligence interviewees and the prior 1865 family letter

Previous Phase 53 report traced William Cunningham's taking of **three unnamed men** to Commodore Saltonstall while the American fleet operated near Fox Islands (July 24–25, 1779), as related in U.S. Marine Corps military history. **These men were not identified as Thomas Beverage or established Fox Islands residents**. Focus first on original Cunningham/*Hazard* journals or Saltonstall's correspondence, not imagination.

The American Ancestors Boston Tea Party biography quotes an **1865 Albert Beverage family letter** claiming Thomas participated in the 1773 Tea Party and privateered. This establishes a nineteenth-century **family statement**, not participation. The original letter is **still not obtained**. Museum and Massachusetts Archives requests were sent by the owner and must not be duplicated without explicit authorization.

Also important: the **1781 *Fly*** is a **different operation** from the **1776 Marblehead *Polly***, and the **1779 *Charming Polly*** in the Castine expedition vessel list is not automatically the same vessel as the 1776 *Polly*.
