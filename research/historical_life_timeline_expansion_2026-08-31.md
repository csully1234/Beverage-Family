# Historical life and timeline expansion — 2026-08-31

## Scope and method

This pass started from merged `main` after PR #6: 180 effective profiles, 108 effective timeline events, and 14 research notes. It did not attempt to add new relatives. Instead it built a systematic queue of historical/deceased profiles and searched for substantive life history using official legislative records, municipal/town histories, institutional archives, contemporary newspapers and detailed obituaries.

The public-data threshold remained **Verified** or **Strongly Supported**. Interesting claims that could not clear that threshold were preserved as research leads instead of being promoted.

## Strongest newly documented stories

- **James Beveridge Jr.** appears in the first formal Topsham town government in 1764 and later served as a selectman.
- **Thomas Beverage** served nine consecutive years as Vinalhaven town clerk, 1790–1798; **John Beverage** succeeded him for fourteen consecutive years, 1799–1812.
- **John Beverage** and other Vinalhaven inhabitants petitioned the Massachusetts House in 1808 over the proposed relocation of Hancock County courts.
- **Benjamin Kent Beverage** served repeatedly as a Vinalhaven selectman, represented Vinalhaven at Maine’s 1819 Constitutional Convention, authored a surviving December 20, 1819 history of Vinalhaven, and represented the island group in the Maine House in 1823.
- **Nathaniel Winslow Beverage** served in local office and represented Vinalhaven in the Maine House in 1828.
- An **1848 Maine act** authorized a toll bridge across the head of Pulpit Harbor from Enos Cooper land to land belonging to **James and Josiah Beverage**, with a draw for vessels. The statute proves the land/infrastructure context but not final construction.
- **Orris Lyford Beverage** is documented in Colby University’s Class of 1887 and as principal of the Hallowell high school.
- **Frank Beverage** appears in the Congressional Record as a 1912 North Haven petitioner, in 1926 as town treasurer handling water-loan bonds, and in 1927 as a Pulpit Harbor auctioneer.
- **Harold H. Beverage** is documented by IEEE as a teenage wireless experimenter who received signals from Carpathia after the Titanic disaster.
- **Samuel Hiram Beverage** now has a substantially richer World War II communications-service narrative and a documented role in establishing the North Haven Historical Society after his 1973 return.
- **Gerald Gibson Beverage** now has education, Pacific naval service, Central Maine Power and Augusta civic-service history.
- **John Miller Beverage** now has a documented Guatemala missionary career and later seminary/library work.
- **Norwood P. Beveridge Jr.** now has a documented Harvard/legal/teaching career and a clearly sourced COVID-19 death.
- Later collateral profiles were enriched with real working-life history: lobstering, carpentry, textile work, poultry farming, agricultural packing/canning, school food service, teaching, sewing/manufacturing and community volunteering.

## Mercury/tannery story

The base archive contains a striking family tradition that Jemima Crabtree Beverage (1837) and later James Beverage and daughter Lucy C. Beverage (December 1850) died from mercury/mercurial exposure connected with James’s tannery. Those event descriptions are preserved unchanged in the protected/base timeline, but their causal claim currently traces to a cemetery-derived source.

Targeted searches were run for the people, dates, North Haven/Vinalhaven, tannery/tanning, mercury, mercurial, quicksilver, poisoning and hemorrhage variants, including historical newspaper material and the 1889 Vinalhaven history. This pass did **not** locate an independent contemporaneous or official source proving mercury poisoning. Research-event overlays therefore mark the cause as a **Research Lead / unverified cause**, while preserving the story for future investigation.

## High-value unresolved historical leads

1. **Mercury/tannery deaths** — locate original death/coroner/physician/church records or the source behind the cemetery narrative before treating the cause as verified.
2. **Benjamin Crabtree Beverage diphtheria claim** — the base profile says he died in a diphtheria epidemic, but this pass did not locate independent evidence strong enough to upgrade the claim.
3. **Civil War sailor index leads** — records surfaced for Beverage/Beveridge sailors including Lyford/Nathan/Edgar variants, but identity-to-profile bridges were not secure enough for publication.
4. **1848 North Haven Bridge Company** — the statute proves authorization and Beverage-family land at the bridge terminus; deeds, town minutes or newspapers are still needed to establish construction, ownership changes and operational history.
5. **Gerald G. Beverage World War II vessel** — his obituary contains a ship identification that should be independently checked against naval records before it is repeated as fact; campaign/service claims are retained at obituary-supported level without asserting the vessel.
6. **Unindexed regional newspapers** — Rockland, Belfast, Vinalhaven and island newspapers likely contain additional accidents, vessels, court matters, property transfers and business notices not exposed by current web OCR.

## Privacy and protected material

No new living-person profiles, addresses, phone numbers or private contact details were added. `data/people.json` and `data/events.json` remain untouched. Existing residence arrays and protected historical/property event records remain unchanged; all historical enrichment is layered through research overlays.
