# Phase 23 — Matthew's original probate child designations and deed-chain research

*19 October 2026 — source-critical follow-up to the 31 scans received from the Waldo County probate office. Research IDs below refer to the earlier **analyst-assigned** image order, not original court page numbers.*

## Documentary upgrade: eight heirs expressly called Matthew's **children**

**Review scan 09** is an October 1837 order of Waldo County Probate Judge Alfred Johnson to divide the real estate belonging to Matthew Beverage, late of Hope, **“to and among his children viz.”** The manuscript line immediately following enumerates eight names also present in **review scan 31**, Ezekiel's estate petition dated September 1837:

1. Achsa Heald
2. James M. Beverage
3. Polly Tucker
4. Thomas J. Beverage
5. Sarah A. Beverage
6. George W. Beverage
7. Martha Beverage
8. Ezekiel G. Beverage (additional middle initials uncertain)

The September petition separately establishes inheritance interests; the **October warrant uses the explicit child relationship**. Unlike the prior summary saying only that the eight were heirs, the court's own wording is direct evidence of Matthew as their father. It remains evidence of the **legal/social parent-child relationship, not an independently demonstrated genetic relationship**, nor evidence that Clarissa was the mother of any particular child.

**Review scan 10**: Clarissa of Hope's estate-administration petition is dated 6 March 1837 and reports Matthew's death on **14 February 1837**. Review scans 7, 18 and 24 document Clarissa as widow. Her birth surname Cobb still rests on a separate Eaton family narrative, not on these probate forms.

### How the main family tree changes

Ten court-supported people are now introduced with stable IDs: Matthew, Clarissa, and the eight children. The eight children have **only Matthew as their named parent**. Clarissa and Matthew are linked as spouses because contemporary court papers establish the widow relationship, **not** that Clarissa is the mother of all eight. No asserted connection is made between Matthew and Capt. Thomas Beverage, the 1785 Fox Islands minor or Matthew's own proposed parents. Earlier reports by Cyrus Eaton (1851/1865) remain separately attributed and are **not** treated as probate-certified marriages, birthdates or surnames.

### Short source transcription (court scan 09)

> You are hereby directed and empowered to make a just and equal division or partition of the Real Estate whereof Matthew Beverage late of Hope in the county of Waldo, yeoman, died seized and possessed, to and among his children viz.

The child list is in handwritten ink, and the official scan has clearer letters than the earlier secondary history. The court date is the sixth day of October 1837, preceded by Ezekiel's September petition. The original docket number and archival register folio were **not** supplied.

### Selected transcription (court scan 10)

> Matthew Beveridge late of Hope died on the 14 day of February A.D. 1837

This is a direct historical **petition allegation** by Clarissa, not an inspected Maine vital-registration certificate.

### Selected transcription (court scan 18)

> to Clarissa Beverage the said deceased's widow, one full third part of the said estate (so as may be convenient for her) for her dower or thirds, during her natural life

This records the legal instruction in the probate process. It should not be turned into a precise current property boundary or fee-simple ownership.

## Land investigation: 1837 appraisers' report, review scan 02

The appraisal describes a **homestead farm** and further lots. The midsection appears to include separate properties remembered as purchased from:

- **Nathaniel Alford** (tentative surname reading, potentially important as a deed grantor);
- a person named **Henry** (surname not yet reliable);
- **Lucy Whitney** (tentative reading; wooded lot);
- **George [surname unread]** (another wooded tract).

The report's numerical acreages, prices and one or two surnames are not yet clear enough for confident citation. These are **appraisers' retrospective descriptions**, not actual registered deeds. Names in a later town directory or on a historic map cannot uniquely establish the same seller or a kinship. The land cannot yet be mapped to a modern address.

The original scan, not the reconstructed prose, is decisive for verifying the spelling and number. *No specific deed to Matthew has yet been identified in a bound registry volume.*

## Actual repositories and research method

**Early Hope land title** may appear in different jurisdictions by recording date. Do not presume the jurisdiction based on Hope's present county:

| Coverage | Verified source collection | Next record-level action |

| Hope early-lot system | [Hope Historical Society's 2022 report](https://hopehist.com/Himages/XMay22.html) on 1799 John Harkness original-lot mapping and 1821 taxpayers | Identify lot number from a verified deed before relating any parcel to the historical map. |
| Historical composite maps | [Walsh History Center collection, WHC 2018.43](https://hub.catalogit.app/walshhistorycenter/folder/entry/materials-and-maps-related-to-camden-settlement) | Compare verified metes-and-bounds with a 1799 map and later map overlay; no present-day geolocation inferred yet. |

These are **verifiable access paths**, not evidence that any specific Matthew deed has been retrieved. Search engine results include other people named Nathaniel Alford and do not establish they are the same 1837 grantor.
