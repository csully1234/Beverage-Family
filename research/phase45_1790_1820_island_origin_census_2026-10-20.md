## New evidence and research value

### The 1790 Thomas *Beverege* household was eleven people, not an identified family of eleven

A transcription of the federal government's 1908 published `Heads of Families at the First Census ... Maine`, Vinalhaven town, **printed page 32**, reports **Thomas Beverege** (that spelling) with the five standard 1790 enumerated category totals:

| Category | Number |
| --- | ---: |
| Free white males aged 16 and older **including the head** | **2** |
| Free white males under 16 | **4** |
| Free white females, all ages | **5** |
| All other free persons | **0** |
| Enslaved persons | **0** |
| **Total people enumerated in household** | **11** |

Source: [Maine Genealogy Archives, 1790 Vinalhaven original-era census transcribed from 1908 federal print](https://archives.mainegenealogy.net/2006/10/1790-census-vinalhaven-hancock-county.html). Original enumeration route: **National Archives microfilm M637, roll 2; Vinalhaven manuscript begins p. 58** in the [Maine 1790 Hancock County guide](https://www.mainegenealogy.net/census_guide.asp?censusyear=1790&county=hancock).

**Caution:** The printed book is a retrospective official federal census transcript, NOT the handwritten 1790 original read here. The other ten household inhabitants are unnamed. They are not necessarily children, spouses or even relatives. Name/age compatibility with Capt. Thomas Beverage (b. circa 1750) does not establish this household's exact indexed profile, nor the 1790 adults' relationship to Matthew, James, John White or other alleged children. No family tree edits were made.

### A full original census manuscript REEL located for 1820 Vinalhaven

The National Archives Fourth Census **M33, roll 34**, Cumberland (part) and Hancock counties, survives in a directly available [Internet Archive 194-frame microfilm scan](https://archive.org/details/populationsc18200034unit), id **`populationsc18200034unit`**. Internet Archive item metadata directly verifies NARA reel 0034 and the counties.

Maine's [1820 Hancock County manuscript guide](https://www.mainegenealogy.net/census_guide.asp?censusyear=1820&county=hancock) places **Vinalhaven at original manuscript p. 657**. Independently, the [2019 town-head index](https://www.heirloomsreunited.com/2019/09/vinalhaven-maine-in-1820-census-then-in.html) says original **sheets 657–664** and lists **John, Nathaniel, Thomas and Benjamin Beverage** as heads (among others).

**Unresolved:** The in-browser archive reader requires JavaScript; the archive's complete 194-page downloadable PDF redirected to a host not accessible through the current researcher environment. I **could not see** the original hand-written census sheets, age tallies or the positions of individual heads, and will not invent an Internet Archive BookReader frame for manuscript page 657. Eight original manuscript sheets is NOT eight zero-indexed book-reader frames; it may take human navigation through the reel. The original full-reel link is directly accessible to family visitors for research. No household ages or extra people are stated for 1820.

### A scholarly 2025 study actually explains how settlers reached the islands

`The Maine Genealogist`, **47(4), November 2025**, printed **pp. 170–182**, publishes `The Ebenezer Eames Family: Early Settlers of North Fox Island, Maine` by **David Flanagan and Barbara Beverage Reeves**. [Publisher-hosted PDF](https://maineroots.org/images/Nov25TMG2.pdf), article begins at PDF image page **28 of 75** (zero-indexed page 27), title/intro read via publisher's text and original PDF page 171 visibly reviewed.

The authors cite:

- **Plymouth County, Massachusetts Deeds 58:29**, recording a **15 May 1769** sale by **Ebenezer Eames, of Fox Islands**, of his Marshfield family homestead to James Baker, with a later October 1773 dower release by Margaret. This is a strong early-islands *contextual* origin clue for the EAMES settlers, **not evidence that an ancestor named Beverage was from Marshfield**. The deed was not independently opened.
- **1 June 1785** Fox Islands settlers' Massachusetts petition, cross-referenced to Norwood P. Beveridge's `The North Island: Early Times to Yesterday` (1989), printed **pp. 21–25**; already catalogued and not duplicated as a new original in this project.
- **Rufus Putnam report dated 18 March 1786, paper Box 001, Maine State Archives**, cited precisely at the journal's printed p. 170, note 6. **This box and the petition are ALREADY catalogued in Phase 30**; the new publication improves contextual understanding, but is not a second independent manuscript. No settler-specific Matthew original text inspected.
- **1800 Vinalhaven census extra question**, where residents identified "from whence emigrated", documented in Walter Goodwin Davis, `Part of Hancock County, Maine, in 1800`, *NEHGR* **105 (1951):204–206 and continuation pp. 276–291**. General island settlers give Marshfield, Cape Cod and Martha's Vineyard among their origins. **No particular Beverage head's original migration answer has been read**, so no migration place is filled into a Beverage profile. This is the most promising overlooked *actual household-level clue* for next research.

Only citation, short independent summary, source links and original-document search instructions are included on the family website. No article PDF, graphics or lengthy passages were reproduced.
