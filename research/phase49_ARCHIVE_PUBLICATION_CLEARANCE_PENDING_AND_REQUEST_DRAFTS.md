# Publication and archive-access gate for the seven original Fox Islands scans

**Status (2026-10-26): drafted / publication clearance not yet documented.** The project's seven archival TIFFs and photo-derived JPGs have been prepared for the family archive's new manuscript reader. This technical preparation is **not proof of permission to publish.**

Massachusetts Archives official Reference Services rules state that archival materials may be used in publications at no additional fee, but readers **must obtain a Permission to Publish form** from Reference staff, use their archival citation, and furnish a copy of the completed publication. Their policy distinguishes Massachusetts-government-created records (not copyrighted) from materials submitted by outside individuals (whose copyrights may be separate). Source: https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/research/research-services.htm

Subject: Permission to publish seven Fox Islands petition/resolve manuscript scans, SC1 Series 228 Ch. 97

I am preparing a nonprofit family-history research website covering early North Haven/Vinalhaven (Fox Islands), Maine. I have seven TIFF copies of the 1785 inhabitants' petition and 1786 legislative resolve from the Massachusetts Archives, SC1 / Series 228, Passed Resolves, February 1785 Session, Chapter 97 (four petition leaves, three resolve leaves).

Could you please provide the Archives' Permission to Publish form and your required credit/citation wording for displaying optimized page images online at https://beverage-family.onrender.com/?page=collection&view=fox-originals, with a visitor page viewer and individual JPG/full-set ZIP downloads? The public source-code repository is https://github.com/csully1234/Beverage-Family.

I would also appreciate guidance on two paleographic discrepancies:
(1) Beside Matthew Beveridge on petition leaf 3, “a minor” is underlined, and a lower note seems to read “major.” Is this known to be a correction or a second annotation?
(2) The original legislative resolve, leaf 1, appears to say “seventy two” qualifying settlers, whereas the 1889 Vinalhaven historical printing reports “seventy-five” in the corresponding clause.

I can provide exact page scans and the draft credit if helpful. Please let me know whether the images already supplied require any additional reproduction terms. Thank you.
